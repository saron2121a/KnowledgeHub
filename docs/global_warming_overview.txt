Global Warming: Causes, Impacts, and Solutions

1. Introduction
Global warming refers to the long-term rise in Earth's average surface temperature due to human activities, primarily the emission of greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. It is a major component of broader climate change.

2. Primary Causes
The main driver of global warming is the enhanced greenhouse effect. Key contributing factors include:
- Burning of Fossil Fuels: Combusting coal, oil, and natural gas for electricity, heat, and transportation releases carbon dioxide (CO2).
- Deforestation: Clearing forests reduces the Earth's capacity to absorb CO2 from the atmosphere through photosynthesis.
- Agriculture and Livestock: Ruminant animals produce methane (CH4), a potent greenhouse gas, while synthetic fertilizers release nitrous oxide (N2O).
- Industrial Processes: Manufacturing and chemical processing release various industrial gases and emissions.

3. Key Impacts
- Temperature Extremes: Increased frequency and intensity of heatwaves.
- Melting Ice and Sea Level Rise: Thermal expansion of seawater and rapid melting of glaciers and ice sheets contribute to coastal flooding and erosion.
- Shift in Weather Patterns: Changes in precipitation lead to severe droughts in some areas and extreme storms/flooding in others.
- Biodiversity and Ecosystem Loss: Disruptions to natural habitats threaten wildlife species with extinction and harm marine life due to ocean acidification.

4. Mitigation and Adaptation Strategies
- Renewable Energy Transition: Replacing fossil fuels with clean energy sources like solar, wind, hydro, and geothermal power.
- Energy Efficiency: Improving industrial equipment, building insulation, and vehicle fuel standards.
- Reforestation and Conservation: Protecting existing forests and planting new trees to sequester carbon.
- Climate Policy and Innovation: International agreements (such as the Paris Agreement), carbon pricing, and investment in carbon capture technologies.
